## 7. The forest — native species around the property

The property sits inside one of the last intact native forests of the
Andean-Patagonian range:

- **Coihue** (*Nothofagus dombeyi*) — the dominant evergreen southern beech
  surrounding the property on every side, reaching up to 40 m.
- **Radal** (*Lomatia hirsuta*) — small understory tree, cream-white blooms
  in summer, gold leaves in autumn.
- **Lenga** (*Nothofagus pumilio*) — deciduous southern beech that turns
  whole mountainsides red in autumn; grows up to the treeline.
- **Ciprés de la Cordillera** (*Austrocedrus chilensis*) — ancient species,
  individual trees can live over 1,500 years; aromatic reddish wood.

## 9. Etymology / context (for color, if guests ask about the name "Patagonia")

The name "Patagonia" first appeared in writing in 1520, recorded by Antonio
Pigafetta during Magellan's voyage. He called the indigenous Tehuelche people
"patagones." Mapuche tradition calls the region "Wallmapu" — the surrounding
territory.
